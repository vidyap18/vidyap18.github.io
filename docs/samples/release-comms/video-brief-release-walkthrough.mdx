# Video brief: Build and Live release walkthrough

<span className="vp-label">Original sample, re-created for a fictional product.</span>

## 1. What we are trying to do

One 90 to 120 second video, released with the release note on rollout day. It takes the biggest shift in this release, the split of every planning model into Build and Live workspaces, and makes it feel clear and reassuring.

**Audience:** every Meridian Studio customer. The video must introduce Build and Live as a genuine improvement and reassure anyone with existing models that nothing has moved.

**Tone:** Meridian at its most confident. Warm, informal, informative without sounding like a webinar, reassuring without sounding defensive. The release note's closing line, "Happy planning", is the voice throughout.

## 2. The plan

Four beats, each introduced by an animated question card. The presenter is on camera for the human beats (intro, the why, reassurance, close) and voices screen capture for the product moments (the Edit/Preview switch, the Promotion panel, the Live link).

| Beat | Card | Runtime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Intro | "Hi, I'm [name]." | 10 sec |
| 2. What is changing? | "So what's changing?" | 30 to 40 sec |
| 3. Two workspaces | "Two workspaces." | 35 to 45 sec |
| 4. Closing | "That's it." | 10 to 15 sec |

## 3. For the presenter: recording

* **Framing.** Chest-up, eyes slightly above the lens, plain background.
* **Takes.** One standalone take per question, two if you can (one formal, one relaxed).
* **Screen capture.** 100% zoom, fresh browser window, no tabs, no dev tools.
* **The words are yours.** The questionnaire gives direction, not a script.

## 4. The questionnaire

### Question 1: the intro

**Direction.** Name, role, one-line setup: "Hi, I'm [name], a product manager at Meridian. Something new lands on Tuesday, and I wanted to walk you through it in under two minutes."
**Tone.** Conversational. A small smile works.
**Watch out for.** "Excited to share" and "thrilled to". Webinar energy.
**Visual.** Presenter to camera. No product.

### Question 2: what is actually changing?

**Direction.** Lead with the why: "We kept hearing that planners were nervous to touch a model while the team was using it." Then the picture: build and check in one place, promote when ready, business users see a version that changes only on purpose. End with a bridge into Question 3.
**Tone.** Informational, genuinely enthusiastic.
**Visual.** Start on the presenter. Pull to screen capture for the Edit/Preview switch and the Promotion panel. Return to the presenter before the next card.

### Question 3: two workspaces

**Direction.** Three moves. Introduce: "Every model now has two workspaces, Build and Live." Explain: "You promote from Build to Live when you are ready, and a readiness check runs first." Reassure: "If you have models today, they are exactly where you left them. Same link, same data. Nothing moves to Live until you say so."
**Tone.** Calm and trustworthy. Warmer on the last line; that is the emotional beat.
**Watch out for.** Do not make Build sound temporary. No "for now", no "eventually everything moves".
**Visual.** Presenter naming the workspaces. Screen capture at the Promotion panel: click Promote, the readiness check passes, the Live link appears. Back to the presenter for the reassurance.

### Question 4: closing

**Direction.** Short and unhurried: "That's the headline. The release note has the rest, a few smaller changes and one or two edge cases. If anything looks surprising, reach out to your customer success manager. Happy planning."
**Tone.** Low-key, warm. End on a half-smile.
**Watch out for.** No "check it out", no "learn more". A heads-up from a product lead, not a promo.
**Visual.** Presenter to camera. No product.

## 5. For the editor: edit notes

* **Balance.** Presenter for the human beats, screen capture for the product, question cards between. Conversational, not promotional.
* **Question cards.** Four, one per beat. They carry the visual personality; the question appears as if being thought, not stamped on.
* **Voiceover continues** over product footage in Q2 and Q3. Return to the presenter at the end of each beat so the viewer re-anchors.
* **Pacing.** Tight cuts. Trim long takes; the cards absorb short ones.
* **Two moments to weight:** the Edit/Preview switch in Q2 and the Live link appearing in Q3. These are the payoffs.
* **Sound.** Light, warm track. Fade fully in the final beat so "Happy planning" lands in silence.
