# I prototype my plans before I build them

<span className="vp-eyebrow">Design prototypes · 2025 to 2026</span>

**What.** When I plan a change to the documentation, such as a new navigation, versioning or a landing page, I build a working prototype of it first: a page with real content that people can click through, not a slide or a written description.

**Why.** A plan in a document leaves people to imagine the result, and everyone imagines something different. A prototype shows stakeholders exactly what I intend, so they react to the same thing, raise concerns early, and agree before I start. Once the plan is agreed, I go ahead with the implementation.

**How.** I build them myself in HTML, CSS and JavaScript, mostly with Claude Code, using real product content so the review is about the actual reading experience. Each one answers one question. I walk the people affected through it, and change it until the decision is clear.

## What this shows

Design thinking applied to documentation: framing the question, making the plan concrete, and getting stakeholders to agree on what they can see before I build it.
