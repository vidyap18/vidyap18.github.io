<div>
<span className="pg-tag pg-tag--accent">Workflow · I set it up</span>

<h3>Why it works</h3>

A pull request on its own explains the code but not the reason. A ticket on its own explains the reason but not what shipped. Together they give a draft that is accurate about behaviour and clear about purpose, so the writer's time goes into checking and explaining, not collecting.

My part was making this the way my team starts a page, and pushing engineering to link every pull request to its ticket, so the two arrive together.

<span className="pg-tag pg-tag--accent">Claude skill · the last check before a page ships</span>

<h3>Retrieval readiness</h3>

Our AI assistant does not read a page the way a person does. It cuts the page into small pieces by heading and answers from whichever pieces match the question. A page can be perfectly clear to a reader and still give the assistant a piece that says "this setting" without naming it, or a fact that only exists inside a table.

The skill reads a page the way the assistant will and checks seven things: unexplained abbreviations, sections that only make sense with the rest of the page, facts that live only in tables, prerequisites far from the steps that need them, limits that are only implied, a term used two ways, and two pages that contradict each other.

When the assistant gives a wrong answer, the same check helps decide what to do next. If the page fails, the writer fixes it. If the page passes, the failed answer is escalated to Engineering to investigate, and we call it a retrieval problem only once they confirm the cause.
</div>
